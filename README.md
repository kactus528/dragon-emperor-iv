# Dragon Emperor IV: The Eternal War

Playable demo of Taiyo's fictional 1997 strategy RPG for the Taiyo Vision 32-bit system.

A miniature war-table kingdom drops into stereoscopic battles of 400 vs 400 sprite soldiers on a 144x80 battlefield, drawn as instanced sprite batches. View it in mono, red/cyan, side-by-side or cross-eye stereo on a desktop, or in WebXR on a Meta Quest (open the GitHub Pages link in the Quest Browser and press Enter VR).

New players get an nine-page **How to play** guide when they press Start, covering the war table, match-ups, orders, magic, duels, spoils and the view controls. Every page lists both keyboard and Quest 2 controls, with a controller diagram that lights up the buttons that page uses (the Quest list comes first when opened in the Quest browser). It can be skipped, the "Don't show this at start" choice is remembered, and it can be reopened from **How to play** on the title screen. It works on desktop and in VR.

## Tactical rules (the game since Build 127)

As of Build 127 Tactical is simply how the game plays: the title-screen toggle is gone and every battle and war uses these rules, single player and multiplayer alike. The classic rules are still in the code (Build 126 and the build-119 branch play them) but are no longer offered.

**Squads.** Each army is split into up to eight squads, each with its own **cohesion** (blue bar) and **morale** (green/gold/red bar), shown on a marker above it. The marker letter shows its state: `!` wavering, `X` routing, `>` charge at full speed, `~` re-forming, `H` hidden, `.` an order on its way.
- Cohesion falls with casualties, flank and rear blows, broken charges, spells and formation changes, and recovers out of contact (faster near the general). A disordered squad hits softer and takes more damage.
- Morale falls with losses, low cohesion, being surrounded and nearby routs, and recovers out of contact and near the general. Below 35 a squad **wavers** (slower, hits softer); held below 12 for 1.5 s it **routs** and runs, and every friendly squad nearby loses heart. A general close by halves that shock and can **rally** a routing squad once if he reaches it within a few seconds. Army morale is now the average of its squads.
- **Leadership:** a general steadies squads within about 11-15 units (more at higher level, less when wounded). A wounded or distant general also makes orders slower to arrive.

**Combat.**
- **Facing and flanks:** each squad faces its nearest enemy squad, and in melee it turns only slowly. Blows from the side do +25% (+40% against a Wedge, +15% against Spread, +10% against Knights) and from behind +50%, and both shred cohesion and morale. A Square has no flanks.
- **Frontage:** only the soldiers who can reach an enemy fight; a fourth attacker on the same man adds little (a third in a narrow pass). Outnumbering the enemy locally adds up to +35%.
- **Charges:** a squad on Charge builds momentum over distance (cavalry, knights and dragonriders up to full power over 12 units, others half as much over 20). The first impact hits much harder and staggers the target. Woods, bog and fords stop momentum building, halting wastes it, and a flank blow or a Thunder Lance bolt breaks it. A charge into the front of a Square breaks: the attackers lose cohesion and fall back for 4 s to recover, and the computer remembers not to try it again for a while. Cavalry wheel away after a few seconds of melee and charge again.
- **Formations:** Line fights at full frontage (+10%); Wedge hits harder when charging but its flanks are exposed; Square stops charges but is slow; Spread halves spell damage but suffers against concentrated attacks (+20-30%). Changing formation now takes about 3-7 s (less for a high-level general and steady troops), during which squads lose cohesion and take 15% more damage. You can't change again until it is done.
- **Match-ups are conditional:** spears only beat horse when they are steady and facing the charge; horse only rides down archers, mages and monks with momentum or from the flank; archers only beat infantry at range.
- **Troop behaviour:** archers suppress what they hit (slower, shakier) and step back from melee unless ordered to Charge; mages' bolts disorder; dragonriders frighten; knights resist disorder; ranged troops shooting downhill reach further and hit harder.
- **Spells:** every spell hit costs cohesion and morale. Starfall and Ash Rain do extra damage to packed formations; Ash Rain leaves burning ground for 6 s; Arrow Storm suppresses; Thunder Lance breaks charges; Dragon's Breath scatters squads from the flames; Eclipse doubles the enemy's order delay and halves how far the computer can see.
- **Concealment:** a squad standing still in woods with no enemy within 11 units hides (the enemy cannot see or target it). It is revealed when an enemy comes within 9 or when it attacks, and its first 3 s of attacks after an ambush hit 30% harder.
- The computer gets no hidden damage or mana bonus in Tactical mode; difficulty comes from how well it fights (see below). The armies tire after 75 s instead of 90.

**Orders.**
- Orders now take time to reach squads: about 0.2-2 s depending on the general's distance and health, and the squad's cohesion. Charge and Retreat commit a squad for 3 s.
- **Single squads:** on a flat screen press **Tab** (Shift+Tab back) to select a squad, or click on the squad itself; **0** or clicking it again returns to the whole army. Order keys then go to that squad only, and clicking the field sends it there (it holds on arrival). In VR, point at one of your squads and tap **A** to select it, tap A on the field to move it there, and the left trigger's orders go to it; tap A on the squad again to deselect.
- **Flank** now sends squads around the side of a real enemy squad, then into its side or rear.
- Warnings appear when a squad is wavering, surrounded, routing, rallying, or its charge breaks.

**Generals and duels.**
- Generals are picked **blind**: the computer chooses without seeing your choice, guessing from your last general and your strongest. The reveal banner names its personality.
- Each enemy general has a **personality** (Aggressive, Cautious, Defensive, Opportunistic or Balanced) that shapes its battle plan and its duel habits: an Aggressive one tends to go Strike, Strike, Power; a Defensive one Guard, Guard, Strike; a Cautious one alternates Guard and Strike; an Opportunistic one counters your last move.
- A duel only starts if the target general is thinly guarded (fewer than 10 of his soldiers within 7 units) or is seeking the duel himself; otherwise his bodyguard keeps the generals apart and a seeking general stops short. Each duel exchange moves both armies' morale a little; the winner's squads gain 20 morale and the loser's Berserk meter empties. **Leaders are never left alone:** in Tactical mode the rule applies to your leader too. Your leader will not march without another general, someone must stay with him in a castle, and he fights last in battle (send another general first). Computer rulers never join a blockade. A general who **starts** a duel and loses also costs his side's other companies 15% of their troops to desertion.

**Crystals and events.**
- Crystals show as a see-through ghost for 10 s before they can be taken, with a small pie timer over each that fills as it forms; when the pie is full the crystal turns solid and glows, and are claimed by holding the ground beside them: four or more soldiers within 2 units, outnumbering the enemy there 1.5 to 1, for 2.5 s. Soldiers channelling a crystal stop fighting. Their effects are smaller: fury and the Cyclone and Thundercloud last 5-6 s, Meteor Shower 3 s, and reinforcements are halved.
- Battle events are announced about 4 s ahead (birds burst from the woods, horns, a shadow overhead, distant thunder), and ambushes and sellsword bands are half as big.

**The computer commander** works at squad level: it sees only what is within sight of its squads (not hidden ones), throws a squad at any enemy squad whose side is exposed while it is fighting to the front, piles onto wavering, disordered or isolated squads, keeps cavalry from charging through woods and bog, on Knight and Warlord sets the army's order itself (heavy troops charge rather than wait to be charged, archers hold, foot charges unless outnumbered and forms Square against incoming horse), keeps a reserve behind the line (except an Aggressive general) and commits it to a flank once the lines lock or to plug a breaking squad, and adapts: if it keeps being flanked it holds its end squads, and if it keeps being charged it forms Square. On Squire it rethinks every 3 s and often misses chances; on Knight every 1.6 s; on Warlord every 0.8 s and rarely misses.

**War table.**
- Every road and castle has a battlefield type, written on the road, and battles there use it (unless you picked a battlefield), so you can choose where to fight.
- **Blockades:** select a marching army and press **Blockade** (Y in VR) before it arrives. It then camps outside an enemy castle instead of storming it. Each week the garrison loses 15% of its troops, some health and loyalty, and sometimes a level of fortification, while the besiegers lose 3%. A starved-out castle opens its gates. Press **Storm the castle** whenever you're ready. A blockading army carries a flashing BLOCKADE sign, and you get a warning when one camps outside your castle; attack it to break the blockade. A computer defender sallies out when it is stronger, and computer attackers blockade strong castles half the time and give up after four weeks: they storm if they are at least four-fifths as strong as the garrison, otherwise they go home.
- **Balance of power:** when one nation holds 45% or more of the claimed castles (at least 8), the other two stop attacking each other and gang up on it.
- **Succession:** capturing a ruler no longer ends a nation. The highest-level free general takes the crown; a quarter of the others ride off to rival nations, loyalty drops and half the treasury is lost. The captured ruler becomes an ordinary prisoner who can be ransomed. A computer nation with two castles or fewer formally surrenders instead, and a nation with no free generals falls. This applies to you too: losing Kaelen hands the crown to your best general.
- In a two-player war the third nation stays on the map under computer control.

**Skirmish** gains an **Army ratio** setting (1:1, 1:1.5, 1:2, 1:3) that shrinks your army, for testing whether tactics can beat numbers. After every Tactical battle the results list a **Tactics** line: flank and rear blows, charges that struck home, broke on a square or were disrupted, and routs and rallies on each side.

## Difficulty
Pick one on the title screen (left trigger in VR):
- **Squire:** enemy troops hit 22% softer, enemy magic recharges slowly, the rivals mostly fight each other, Duskmarch keeps a 10-week truce, your garrisons recover fast, and Kaelen is always rescued if captured.
- **Knight (default):** enemy troops hit 10% softer, the enemy counter-picks less often, the rivals target each other as well as you, Duskmarch keeps a 6-week truce, your generals level faster, and Kaelen's bodyguard rescues him if he's captured.
- **Warlord:** the original tuning. Both rivals hunt you first, and losing Kaelen ends the war.

**Ludicrous gore** (checkbox under the slider) ignores the slider: every death leaves big splatters and a fountain of droplets, and about one death in sixteen erupts in a huge blood explosion that paints the ground around it. **Blood** is set with the slider on the title screen (0 = off, remembered between visits). Every soldier who dies leaves a splatter on the ground where he fell, with a spray of droplets at higher settings; higher settings make splatters bigger and more frequent. Splatters stay for the whole battle (up to about 680 at once) and clear when the next battle begins.

## Weekly castle tasks and loyalty

Select one of your castles to set its task for the coming weeks (in VR, press Y with the castle selected to cycle):
- **Rest** (default): generals there recover 50% faster.
- **Search**: each week there is a 22% chance to turn up a relic or one of three wandering generals: Garrick the Sellsword (Infantry), Lyra Swiftwind (Harpies) and Tor Ironhoof (Cavalry).
- **Fortify**: raises the walls one level a week, up to 3; each level adds 120 HP to the gate in sieges. Recovery is slower while the masons work.
- **Reward**: +15 loyalty a week for every general there.

Every general but Kaelen has **loyalty** (♥ in the castle panel). Wins raise it, losses and idle weeks lower it, and a relic gift gives +20. A general under 40 is restless; under 25, he may defect to a neighbouring enemy castle. Enemy castles fortify too, slowly.

## New Game+: the Ages

Unite the realm and, after the battle's results, the war table shows the victory screen with **Begin Age II** (in VR a large board appears: **A** begins the next Age, **X** starts over at Age I; the trigger is ignored there so you can't skip it by accident). A story card opens the new Age, and:
- Every Aldmere general who survived keeps their level, XP, relic and loyalty; wandering generals you recruited return with you; the vault carries over.
- Enemy generals gain 2 levels per Age (up to 10) and increasingly carry relics; enemy castles start with stronger walls.
- The enemy hits harder, recharges magic faster and counter-picks more each Age.
- New battle conditions appear: **Blizzard** from Age II (slower troops, shorter archer range, snow and white-out) and **Blood Moon** from Age III (everyone deals 25% more damage under a red sky).

Progress is saved in the browser: the title screen shows **Continue · Age N**, and losing restarts the current Age with the same carried-over generals. **Start over at Age I** wipes it.

## Boss battles

- **The Ashen Wyrm.** In any battle where Vorgrath fights, his dragon is a boss with its own health bar. Every 7-11 seconds it either swoops with fire across your densest ranks or **lands**: the landing stomp flattens nearby troops, and for about 4 seconds it sits on the field breathing fire while your soldiers, archers and mages can attack it. Your spells also wound it whenever it is low (landing, grounded or diving). Slay it and the enemy's morale collapses, Vorgrath loses his Dragon's Breath for the rest of the battle, and the campaign rewards the **Wyrmheart** relic (+20% spell power, +10 HP, +1 spell charge).
- **Queen Ysolde's Eclipse** (costs 2 charges, aimed like any spell): a black sun with a violet corona rises over the spot you aim at and the whole field goes dark for 7.5 seconds. The other side deals 30% less damage and loses morale, and dark lightning keeps striking soldiers under the black sun about twice a second (it drifts to follow the thickest ranks). The HUD shows YOUR ECLIPSE or ECLIPSE with the seconds left, and a banner counts the kills when it passes. When Ysolde fights for you, it works the same against the enemy.

**Resting:** on Squire and Knight, generals who lose a battle must rest for 1 week (never longer) before they can march again, while winning and marching need no rest at all. On Warlord the old, harsher rules apply: 2 weeks after any battle (won or lost, and after falling back from a defeat) and 1 week after arriving from a march. Resting generals show *Resting N wk* in the castle panel (RESTING on the VR party board) and can't be ticked for a party, but they still defend their castle if it's attacked. The weekly report tells you who is rested and ready. The AI factions follow the same rule.

**Prisoners after a battle:** every captured general gets one recruitment attempt when the battle ends, whichever side wins. If you win, each enemy general you captured either swears loyalty and joins you at the castle you hold (about 60% on Knight, a little better when you've just taken their castle, 10% worse if they already turned you down during the battle) or refuses and is executed. If you lose, each of your captured generals either goes over to the enemy and joins their army (25% + the difficulty's turn rate) or refuses and is executed (Kaelen keeps his own rescue rule). Battles between the two AI factions work the same way. Faction rulers are never recruited; they stay prisoners.

**Surrender:** the general-selection screen always ends with a surrender option: *Surrender the castle* when you defend, *Abandon the siege* when you attack, *Withdraw from the field* on the road (*Concede* in skirmish). Pick it twice to confirm. The battle ends as a defeat, but nobody else is risked: every general you still have forms an army on the war table and walks back to your nearest castle along the roads with all of his troops (prisoners the enemy already took stay prisoners). The same now goes for any army that loses a battle, yours or the AI's: the survivors march home instead of appearing there. A retreating army can't be caught on the road, passes through castles on its way, and turns toward another castle if its destination falls before it arrives. Click it and give it an order to turn it back into a normal army. With one of your armies selected, clicking any castle always sends it there (castle clicks win over nearby army markers), and the army turns on the spot along the shortest road from exactly where it stands.

**Retreat** scatters your soldiers toward the nearest edge of the field (any edge); everyone who gets off the field is saved. Your general heads for the nearest edge along a real path (around rocks, hills and rivers), and you can steer him exactly like a fleeing general: left stick in VR, WASD on desktop. He sprints by himself when enemies get within a few steps, and if something pins him for two seconds he squeezes past it. You can still cast while retreating (an empty-magic trigger pull no longer switches you to Charge), and when your general is fleeing after a lost round, the right trigger casts your spell (or fires your storm) when it's ready and sprints otherwise, while the left trigger always sprints. When he reaches any edge the round ends: he escapes with the soldiers who slipped away plus 60% of those still fighting, and can fight again in a later round. The enemy can escape the same way.

## Gold, the market and siege gear

Every nation has gold (you start with 60). The amount shows in the war-table bar and at the top of the VR panel.

- **Earning it:** each castle pays 8 gold a week (a capital 14). Winning a battle pays 30 gold, plus 1 per 8 enemy soldiers killed, 35 per general captured and 50 for taking or holding the castle in dispute; the loser gets a small consolation (1 per 16 kills). Computer nations earn 60% of that on Squire, 85% on Knight and 110% on Warlord.
- **Spending it:** select one of your castles or armies. On the flat page the market is at the bottom of the left-hand panel; in VR a Market board appears to the right of the war table (point and pull the trigger).
- **The payout:** on the results screen your winnings spin up on slot-machine reels after the spoils are listed: the digits whirl, lock one at a time from the left with a clang, then flash gold with a fanfare and show your new treasury. It appears on the VR results board too, and the trigger still skips it.

- **Battle plan:** right after the taunts, each round, you choose your starting formation (Line, Wedge, Square or Spread); the army moves into it as you highlight each one. On a flat screen click a card or press 1-4; in VR the list is on the wrist panel (left trigger next, right trigger choose). In multiplayer both commanders plan at once, and anyone who has not chosen after 25 seconds gets a Line.
- **Archers on the wall:** when you defend a castle whose gate still stands, the plan screen then asks whether to man the wall: none, 15% or 30% of your soldiers. Those soldiers become archers on the ramparts whatever your general's troop type. Their arrows reach a little over a quarter of the way down the battlefield (28% of its length, about four and a half times an ordinary archer's range) and hit 25% harder, take 60% less damage, and foot soldiers cannot reach them (enemy archers, mages, flyers and spells can). They are also the ones who pour boiling oil: with no archers on the wall near the gate, oil you bought goes unused. If the gate is broken or the wall is breached they come down and fight as ordinary archers. Computer defenders always man their wall (10% on Squire, 20% otherwise, 25% if they have oil).
- **Ramparts:** castle walls now have a flat wall-walk with a crenellated parapet on the outer face, a lintel over the gate and battlements on the gate towers.
- **Castle walls and the landscape:** a castle's gate road is kept clear. If mountains run across the approach, a smooth pass is cut through them down to ground level so the ram and the troops stay in view; woods stop at the wall and away from the gate, and trees standing in the wall or by the gate are removed. Clearing boulders and low walls from the gate road now also frees the ground they stood on, which fixes fleeing generals getting stuck on invisible walls.
- **Long rounds end sooner:** after 45 seconds of a round, crystals start appearing faster and faster (about 7 a minute early on, rising to about 20 a minute past two minutes), with no limit on how many, and they favour fight-ending ones (Meteor Shower, Thundercloud, Cyclone, Berserk Rage, Dread Horn) over ones that drag a fight out (Holy Nova, Resurrection, Reinforcements, Mana Surge).
- **Lava:** lava now has a sharp glowing edge with a black crust rim, and nobody can step on it or stand within about a unit of it. When soldiers fight right beside lava, now and then one is shoved in (with a scream). Castle gates are never placed by lava: any lava pool in a gate's approach is filled in.
- **Duels need open ground:** a duel is always fought where both generals can be seen: on roughly level ground clear of trees, boulders, walls, lava and the field's edge, with nothing (hills, mountains, trees) between the camera and either general. If there is no such ground within 25 units, the duel is put off for a few seconds.
- **Fight!:** the duel only starts once both generals have walked to their marks in front of the camera (anyone still on the way after 7 seconds is brought straight there), then a big FIGHT! sign and a horn open the first exchange.
- **Crowds slow a hunting general:** a general seeking a duel cannot dash when six or more enemy soldiers are packed around him or in his way, moves at half to 70% speed, and is now and then jostled to a crawl. With a clear road ahead he runs and dashes at full speed.
- **Cursor on walls:** the battlefield cursor now sits on top of a castle wall when you point at it, instead of sinking through.
- **Spell-ready cursor:** the cursor, laser and wrist hint only light up when you have enough magic for your general's spell (Dragon's Breath and Eclipse cost 2 charges).
- **Switch generals on the fly (Build 171):** the left trigger in VR (Tab on desktop, or the "Switch to …" button) instantly swaps your general for the next one waiting, right where you stand, with no menu and no pause. The general you switched out steps back into the waiting line with his surviving troops and his wounds, so you can cycle through your whole party and back. Retreat (B or Y in VR, Esc on desktop) still opens the menu for withdrawing a general for good or leaving the dungeon.
- **Fix: entrance door and pointing at gates (Build 170):**
  - **The door was in the wrong room:** since Build 166 the entrance door was sometimes placed past the first gate, so generals walked in on the far side of a boulder and couldn't push it from there. The door now always goes in one of the entrance hall's own walls.
  - **Pointing at gates in VR:** pointing at a closed gate, or the floor at its foot, now targets the gate itself (the laser turns gold), and A sends the right troops to it from up to 16 units away.
- **Dungeon deaths like the battlefield (Build 169):** soldiers and monsters killed in a dungeon die the way they do in battle. The blow throws them back (spells throw them higher), fliers drop out of the air, armour chips fly, and the body lies still, blinks and fades. Blood follows the same settings: the Blood slider sets how often and how big the stains are, the stains stay on the floor for the rest of the visit, and Ludicrous gore adds sprays, extra stains and the occasional burst.
- **Every dungeon spell rebalanced (Build 168):** Dragon's Breath is a real 11-unit cone that burns everything in front of the general and drives it back. Thunder Lance pierces everything in a 12-unit line, then arcs to the three enemies nearest where it ends. Arrow Storm drops two waves over a wider area. Into a fortress guard group (158 troops), one cast now takes out roughly: Starfall about half, Dragon's Breath 45-55%, Arrow Storm and Thunder Lance about 40%, and Ash Rain about 35%. Eclipse takes about a quarter and stuns, and Mending Light heals. Battlefield spells are unchanged.
- **Stronger dungeon spells (Build 167):** spells in dungeons recharge in 5 seconds instead of 9 and hit about twice as hard (power 30 + 8.5 per level instead of 24 + 3). Blasts throw enemies back from the centre and hit hardest in the middle. Starfall now drops one great star and two smaller ones around it, with a screen shake and a controller rumble. A level-7 Kaelen's Starfall cuts a fortress guard group roughly in half.
- **Dungeon door and retreat/swap (Build 166):**
  - **The door:** each dungeon has a great double door in a wall of the entrance hall, marked EXIT · WAY IN. Every general comes in through it, and it swings open as you approach. Walking up to it asks whether to send your general out.
  - **Retreat or swap, anywhere:** press B or Y in VR, Tab or Esc on desktop, or the "Retreat / swap general" button. The game pauses on a menu listing the generals waiting at the stairs. Pick one and your current general withdraws with his survivors while the new one walks in through the door. You can also **Leave the dungeon**, or **Stay** (B again, or Esc) to carry on. Rival expeditions also leave through the door.
- **VR dungeon view stays put (Build 165):** when you move the dungeon with the grips, it stays where you leave it. The table still slides along to follow your general, but keeps him at the spot you chose instead of snapping back to the default one. Rotating or zooming with the right stick now pivots around your general.
- **Taller, dressed dungeon walls (Build 164):** walls are nearly twice as tall, built of dressed stone courses with a carved frieze and plinth, a cornice along the top, and pilasters every few tiles. Each archetype has its own dressing: red banners in a fortress, glowing rune plaques in a crypt, lava veins in a lair. Walls on the near (camera) side of each room are cut down low, like the front of a diorama, so they never hide your troops. The locked and twin-plate doors are taller to match.
- **Dungeon combat and commands (Build 163):**
  - **Visible arrows:** archers fire real arrows that arc and point the way they fly, and mages fire glowing bolts. Every hit flashes.
  - **Aimed spells:** your spell lands where you aim. In VR that's where the right-hand laser points; on desktop it's the mouse (a gold ring shows the spot). On a phone, press the spell button, then tap the spot. Range is 14 units; aiming further casts as far as the spell reaches.
  - **Redirecting your troops:** in VR, point at the floor and press A to send the squad there. Each new press redirects them, and they march there before picking new fights. Pointing at a gate or sign within reach uses it instead. On desktop, right-click a spot.
  - **Gate labels:** every closed gate shows what it needs ("LOCKED: NEEDS A KEY", "HOLD BOTH PLATES", "NEEDS HEAVY PUSH"...). Pressing A at a twin-plate door sends your squad onto the far plate, so you only have to step on the other.
- **One general at a time, pick-up by touch (Build 162):**
  - **One at a time:** an expedition's generals go in one at a time, in the order you chose them; the rest wait at the stairs. When the one inside falls, is captured, or withdraws (the Withdraw button, Esc twice, or Y twice in VR), the game pauses and you choose who goes in next, or leave the dungeon. The new general starts from the last safe spot his predecessor reached (somewhere with no enemies close), and everything already done stays done. Rival expeditions work the same way: when their general is beaten or pulls back, the next one takes over.
  - **Pick-up by touch:** walking any general or soldier over a key or chest collects it, and reaching the altar claims the sanctum once its guardians are dead. Walking up to a clue sign shows its text. The HUD lists what you've found.
- **Dungeon keys (Build 161):** a key is picked up as soon as any of your generals or soldiers steps on it (rival expeditions too), and a locked door opens by itself when one of your generals or soldiers reaches it while you hold a key. A and E still work on both.
- **Dungeons: rival expeditions, map, Lab play mode (Build 160):**

## Sieges

When a general flees at the end of a round, the gate is thrown open so the winner can chase him down through it; the rest of the wall stays up. If the gate was still standing, it is shut again at the start of the next round with the damage it had.


Every battle for a castle is a siege. A stone wall with towers runs the full width of the field in front of the defenders, with a wooden gate placed in the clearest lane across the field; rocks and ruin walls in that lane are cleared away, and a causeway crosses any water, so the gate can always be reached. Attackers path to the gate and batter it (Knights ×2.2, Dragonriders ×1.8, Mages ×1.4, Infantry ×1.2, Cavalry ×0.6, Archers and Monks barely scratch it). The gate starts at 180 HP plus 120 per level of fortification. Defenders behind the wall take 30% less damage from arrows, bolts and spells, and the defending AI holds the wall while the gate stands. Harpies and Dragonriders fly straight over. When the gate falls the defenders lose heart and the attackers surge. A broken gate stays broken for the rest of the battle. The HUD shows the gate's health. Skirmish can also set up a siege either way round.

## Multiplayer

Up to three players share one war table, one nation each (Aldmere, the Ashen Legion or Duskmarch). With only two players there is no computer nation: the third nation is left off the map, and its castles start empty and unclaimed for either player to take.

- **Starting:** on the title screen press **Multiplayer**. One player presses **Host a war** and gets a five-letter room code; the others type the code and press **Join**. Everyone picks a nation in the room list, then the host presses **Start the war**. Do this on the flat page; press **Enter VR** afterwards if you want to play in the headset.
- **How it works:** the host's game runs the war table and every battle; the other players get a live feed of the castles, armies, soldiers, spells and sounds, and their clicks and buttons are sent back to the host. Players connect directly to each other (WebRTC through the free PeerJS service), so a few strict networks such as some work or school Wi-Fi can block the connection.
- **On the war table:** everyone plays at the same time. You can only select, march, redirect, halt and set tasks for your own nation. Any player can pause.
- **Battles:** when two players' armies meet (on a road or at a castle), those two fight a live battle. Each picks a general at the start of every round, then gives orders, casts spells (Thunder Lance and Arrow Storm are aimed bolt by bolt for both players), sends squads, uses abilities, seeks duels and duels (both pick Strike, Guard or Power) and steers when retreating or fleeing. If you are on the right-hand side of the battle, your screen is mirrored so your army is always on the left. There are no taunts in multiplayer, and the difficulty bonuses the computer gets (extra enemy damage and mana) are switched off between two humans.
- **The third player** watches the battle for now; everything else on the war table waits until it ends. After a battle, each fighter gets their own results screen, and the war resumes once both press Return to the war table (or after 40 seconds).
- **Winning:** a nation falls when it loses its last castle or its ruler (Kaelen, Vorgrath or Ysolde) dies. A captured human ruler can be cut free by their bodyguard on the easier difficulties. The last nation standing wins the war.
- **Voice chat:** hosting or joining a room asks for your microphone, and everyone in the room can then talk, from the lobby onward. It is an open mic. The small bar in the top-left corner has **Mic on/off** and shows each player's name, which turns green while they speak; the game's sound dips a little while someone is talking. With three players, **To: everyone** switches to speaking only to one player (the third hears nothing from you), and that player sees "only to you" next to your name. In VR, hold the left thumbstick click for half a second to turn the mic on or off and hold the right thumbstick click to change who hears you; the status shows at the bottom right of the VR panel. A quick click still does what it did before (ability and squad), but in multiplayer it now fires when you let go. If you refuse the microphone you can still hear the others. Headphones avoid echo.
- **Leaving:** if a player disconnects or goes silent for 15 seconds, the computer takes over their nation.

## Skirmish

**Skirmish** on the title screen sets up a one-off battle: pick up to three generals for each side from all fourteen, the battlefield, the conditions (clear, fog, night, storm or random), a level (as written, all Lv 5 or all Lv 10) and whether generals carry random relics. Nothing carries over to the campaign. Afterwards you can take a rematch with the same setup or go back to the title.

**Flying troops** (harpies and dragons) climb over obstacles instead of passing through them: rocks, desert stone walls, siege walls and gate towers all have a height (trees don't: flyers pass straight through forests), and flyers look ahead and rise smoothly above whatever is in front of them (and over rising ground), then settle back down once past.

**Rapid fire:** with Thunder Lance or Arrow Storm charged, hold the right trigger (or hold the mouse button) and bolts or volleys keep firing at the aim point as fast as they recharge (about 4–5 a second). Enemy generals now cast the same way: a run of aimed strikes, each at one of your soldiers (or now and then at your general), instead of one big swarm. Thunder Lance is a troop-killer, not a general-killer: a bolt only hurts a general on a near-direct hit (within about 1.8 units), does 3 damage instead of 7, and one cast can do a general at most 10 damage in total however many bolts land on him. The same limit applies to enemy generals casting it at yours.

## Relics

Twelve relics, each held by one general: Sunsteel Blade (+2 attack, +25% duel damage), Aegis of Highmere (+2 defense), Heartwood Charm (+12 HP), War Standard (+40 troops), Mana Pearl (+1 spell charge and starts charged), Stormglass Orb (+25% spell power), Duelist's Gauntlet (+1.5 s to choose in duels), Swiftmail (faster stamina), Lion Banner (+15% starting morale), Ember Ring (+10% troop damage), Ward of Ash (-20% magic damage taken) and Crown of Echoes (abilities recharge 30% faster). In the campaign, winning a battle has a 55% chance to turn up a relic (taking Vorgrath always does); it goes to the general who earned the most XP that battle, or to the vault if everyone already carries one. Hand out vault relics from a castle's panel.

## The campaign
A crystal in the faction's colour floats above any castle where a ruler is staying (blue for Kaelen, red for Vorgrath, violet for Ysolde), so you can always see where each crown is. When a ruler marches out, the crystal travels with his army above its banner. The war table is a living map of 18 castles joined by roads. Aldmere (you), the Ashen Legion and Duskmarch all move armies in real time. Click one of your castles (the strongest generals with troops are pre-ticked; tick or untick to choose your marching party of up to five. In VR a castle board appears to the left of the table: point at a general and pull the trigger to tick him), then click any castle on the map and your army marches there along the roads. You can run several campaigns at once: as soon as one army sets off, select another castle and send a second (or third) army while the first is still on the road. Click a marching army (it has a generous click area, and armies win over castles behind them) to change its orders: redirect it to an enemy castle, use Change course to send it anywhere, Halt it on the road and March on later, or Turn back. In VR, point at the army and pull the trigger, then press A for Change course or X to Halt/March on. In VR: point at your castle and pull the right trigger, then point at the target and pull it again; A cancels. Armies that meet on a road clash, and an army that reaches an enemy castle attacks it. Every battle drops into a battlefield that matches the castle's region. Taking a castle grows your territory, and armies claim empty castles along their route. Capturing a nation's ruler makes it collapse; a nation left with no castles scatters. Once no rival holds a castle, the unclaimed castles swear fealty and the realm is yours: the war is won when every castle flies Aldmere's banner, and lost if Kaelen is captured or your last castle falls. Every week (18 seconds), generals resting in castles heal and muster fresh troops. An advisor line always says what's happening and what to do next, a short feed lists recent events, and rings on the map mark castles with ready generals (gold), castles under threat (red) and castles one road from your selection (white). When you have nothing to do, time fast-forwards automatically. Pause with Space or the Pause button, and change the speed up to 4x.

After every battle a results screen tallies enemy slain and soldiers lost, fills each general's XP bar (with a fanfare on level-ups), slams down a rank from S to D, and lists the spoils: castles won, prisoners taken, new ranks. Click or pull the trigger to skip ahead. Generals gain experience from kills, wins, duels and captures. Each level raises their HP, attack and defense, increases how many troops they command (110 at level 0 up to 400), and strengthens their spell. Ranks run from Captain to Warlord.

## How a battle works
Every round opens with the two generals trading taunts: the camera zooms in on each in turn while they speak in a Taiyo-style dialogue box. In VR the diorama glides toward you so each speaking general stands about 75 cm in front of you, then glides back to where you had it when the battle begins. Any trigger, Space or a click skips it.

Each side fields three generals, each leading 400 troops of one type. Spears beat cavalry, cavalry rides down archers, archers shred infantry. You pick a general for each round, the Ashen Legion tries to counter-pick, and survivors carry their losses into the next round. The battle ends when one side runs out of generals.

- **Orders:** Charge (hits harder, takes more), Hold (everyone stops where they stand, fights only what comes close, takes less damage and restores morale), Flank (sweeps around the enemy's sides), Retreat (falls back to the general), Withdraw (pulls your general out with the troops he has left).
- **Battlefields:** pick one on the war table or leave it on Random. Every battle generates a fresh layout, and soldiers path around obstacles with a flow field.
  - *Thornfield Crossing:* a river crossed only at a bridge and fords, a lake, woods, boulders.
  - *Greyfang Highlands:* snow-capped cliff ridges wall off the field; armies funnel through the passes.
  - *Mirewater Fen:* bogs slow everyone, deep pools block the way, reeds and dead trees.
  - *Sunscar Ruins:* dunes cut into lanes by the broken walls of a dead city, with a palm oasis.
  - *Frostmere:* a frozen lake that slows every army, broken through in places.
  - *Eldwood:* deep woods across most of the field and a stream with fords.
  - *Ashen Wastes:* a lava river crossed by two stone bridges, lava pools, embers.
  Woods slow troops (cavalry most) and halve arrow damage to anyone inside. Archers are nimble skirmishers: when a meteor (Starfall or Ash Rain) is coming down near them, about half of them scatter out of the blast. Lightning gives no warning, but after each bolt about half the archers nearby break and spread out, so a storm can't keep hammering the same packed clump.
- **Flying troops:** harpies and wyrms beat their wings and bob in the air all the time, including when standing still.
- **Multi-kills and Berserk:** when a general's spell or strike (Starfall, Ash Rain, Thunder Lance bolts, Arrow Storm volleys, Dragon's Breath, a crystal's meteors) kills two or more soldiers at once, a "+N" number pops up over the kills (gold for you, red for the enemy, bigger for bigger hauls). Every kill, by spells or by troops, fills that side's **Berserk** meter (the red bar under morale; the label shows BERSERK %), and every soldier you lose fills yours at half rate, so the losing side gets there too. It fills at 150 and carries over from round to round within a battle.
- **Berserk spell:** when your meter is full (the bar turns gold and a banner tells you), your next spell is free, even with no mana, and doubled: twice the meteors for Starfall and Ash Rain, twice the bolts or volleys for Thunder Lance and Arrow Storm (with 16 seconds to use them), a wider and hotter Dragon's Breath, a double-length Eclipse with twice the dark bolts, and twice the soldiers raised by Mend. Casting it empties the meter. To save it for a later round, hold off casting. Computer generals get it too: on Squire their meter fills at half speed, their Berserk spell is only 1.5 times as strong, and they use it only when they are losing; on Knight it fills at 80% speed; on Warlord it is the same as yours. Between two human players it is identical for both.
- **Reactive battlefield:** Meteors (Starfall, Ash Rain, the Meteor Shower crystal), Dragon's Breath and wild wyrms set woods on fire. A burning forest roars for about 12 seconds, burns any ground troops and generals inside (both sides), can spread to nearby woods, and then leaves a bare clearing with no slowdown and no arrow cover. Thunder Lance bolts shatter boulders they hit, opening new paths. In a Blizzard, rivers and lakes freeze solid and can be crossed anywhere (slowly); lava stays lava. Every meteor leaves a scorched crater that slows troops crossing it.
- **Battle events:** about a third of fights get a surprise 10 to 22 seconds in: an **ambush** (20 soldiers burst out of a forest near the enemy army), **sellswords** (a band of 24 of Garrick's sellswords, Lyra's harpies or Tor's riders joins whichever side is losing), a **wild wyrm** (a dragon sweeps across the field burning everyone under it), or a **storm** rolling in.
- **Morale:** kills and spells raise it, losses lower it. An army below 20% starts to rout.
- **Formations** (F or the buttons; Y in VR): Wedge smashes Line (+30%) and Spread (+40%) but breaks on a Square (-30%); Square hits Wedges hard, moves slowly and takes 45% more magic; Spread takes about half damage from magic but hits softer. Changing formation takes a moment to settle. The enemy reads your formation and counters it.
- **Abilities** (R; left-stick click in VR), 22 s cooldown: infantry **War Cry** (+morale, +30% damage), cavalry **Thunder Charge** (+60% speed, +25% damage), archers **Eagle Eye** (+50% damage, longer range).
- **Power-ups:** 2 to 5 times per fight a spinning, faceted crystal (the first 3-4 seconds in, the second soon after, then every 7-10 seconds, capped at five) (with a glowing core, sparkles, a halo and a pool of coloured light on the ground) appears between the armies (up to three at once). Nearby soldiers, and every cavalryman, are drawn to it; the first side to touch it claims it and the enemy can't. Every grab is a moment: slow motion, a shockwave ring, a column of light, a fanfare and a cheer. **Thundercloud** (violet) follows the enemy army for 8 seconds, dropping a lightning bolt about once a second. **Meteor Shower** (orange) rains about seven meteors on enemy soldiers over 5.5 seconds. **Cyclone** (pale cyan) is a tornado that rushes into the enemy ranks for 8 seconds and hurls soldiers into the sky (knights only take damage). **Berserk Rage** (amber) sets your army ablaze for 10 seconds: +60% damage, -25% damage taken, a battle cry and a kill count when it ends. **Dread Horn** (red-orange) sends three shockwaves out from your general, shakes enemy morale and frightens a share of their soldiers to death (more of them close by). **Holy Nova** (green) heals your general 50% and every soldier to full, blasts enemies near your general, and calls four pillars of light down on the enemy. **Reinforcements** (white) send 24 fresh soldiers charging in from your edge of the field. **Mana Surge** (blue) fills your magic, resets your spell and ability cooldowns and refills stamina. **Resurrection** (red ☥) raises up to 30% of your starting army from where they fell. **Dragon's Wrath** (gold, rare) is a legendary crystal: it is bigger, announced with a fanfare and marked by a pillar of light, and whoever grabs it summons a dragon that flies across the field burning the soldiers beneath it. The enemy gets the same effects against you if they grab first. Troops on Hold only chase orbs that are very close. **Send a squad** (G then click, or the Send squad button; in VR point and tap **A**): the 12 soldiers who can get there fastest break off, run to that spot (snapping to a crystal if you point near one), grab anything there and then rejoin the fight. You can send a new squad every second, so several crystals can be raided at once (each squad takes the 12 nearest soldiers not already on a run). Squads and soldiers running for a crystal follow a real path to the spot: rocks and stone walls are part of the pathfinding map, so they route around boulder fields instead of walking into them, and a soldier pinned against something for half a second sidesteps free. A squad gives up after 20 seconds.
- **Drama:** kill streaks call out (Carnage, Rampage, Slaughter...) and swing morale; big multi-kills and duel clashes freeze the frame for a beat, and a general falling or an army breaking drops into slow motion. An army down to its last 15% makes a **last stand** (+60% damage, -35% damage taken, won't rout). Routed troops **rally** if their general survives six seconds. After 90 seconds the smaller army starts to tire.
- **Telegraphs:** the enemy general glows and shouts "gathers power!" 1.6 seconds before every spell, so you can scatter (Spread) or strike first. Floating tags above each army show its formation and whether the match-up is STRONG, EVEN or WEAK.
- **Battle conditions:** some battles roll **Fog** (archer range -30%), **Night** (archer damage -20%), **Blizzard** (freezes the water) or **Storm** (random lightning hits both sides; Thunder Lance recharges 60% faster).
- **Generals** fight on the field, take damage, and each has a spell: Starfall, Arrow Storm, Thunder Lance, Ash Rain, Dragon's Breath. MP holds spell charges, and weaker troop types get more magic to compensate: archer generals hold 5 charges, start with 2 and refill one every ~10 s; infantry hold 3, start with 1, ~16 s; cavalry hold 3, start with 1, ~20 s, and gain less MP from kills. Dragon's Breath costs 2 charges. **Thunder Lance** (cavalry) calls a 10-second storm of 8 bolts (more at higher levels): each click, Space or right-trigger pull drops one precise bolt where you aim, so you can snipe the enemy general or pick apart clumps. Unused bolts strike the thickest enemy ranks when the storm ends, and Esc releases them early. **Arrow Storm** (archers) works the same way: one charge gives 7 volleys (more at higher levels), and each click or trigger pull drops a tight barrage of 20 arrows in a small circle where you aim. Higher levels recharge faster, kills add MP, and each cast spends one charge with a 4-second cooldown.
- **Capture:** a general who falls in a duel or to a spell is taken prisoner. When an army breaks, or a general withdraws, he has to run to any edge of the field to escape, pathing around rivers and walls, while the other army chases him (cavalry is best at this). Catch him before he crosses the edge and he's a prisoner; this works both ways. While an enemy general flees you can keep casting at him (Space and click, or aim and pull the right trigger): Starfall and Ash Rain chase him with a tight cluster, Thunder Lance and Arrow Storm bolts and volleys land where you aim, and if your magic brings his health to zero he is struck down and captured on the spot. Generals have a **stamina** bar (STA, under morale): sprints nearly double their speed for a couple of seconds and refill slowly while they jog. Steer your fleeing general with WASD/arrows or the left stick (he still drifts toward his edge, and the stick goes back to sliding the table afterwards), and sprint with Space or the right trigger; enemy generals sprint when chasers get close, and generals seeking a duel burst across long gaps. Between rounds you can try to persuade prisoners to join you with fresh troops, and the enemy will try the same with yours. Capturing Vorgrath ends the battle.
- **Duels:** like Dragon Force, a duel only starts when the two generals meet on the field. Press Seek duel and your general rides out to find his rival; a strong enemy will hunt yours, and a badly outmatched one will avoid you (which costs his army morale). When they meet, the camera swings to a side-on fighting-game view: health bars and portraits at the top, a countdown in the centre, and a LOCKED IN tag the moment either fighter commits. Both moves are revealed together at the clash. Strike beats Power, Guard beats Strike, Power breaks Guard. A gold glow and a rising whine mean he is winding up a Power blow. Run out the clock and you Strike by reflex. The timer is 3 s on Squire, 2.4 s on Knight, 1.8 s on Warlord, and an exchange resolves the moment both fighters lock in. Win three exchanges in a row to charge a **CRITICAL** (shown as ★ by your name) that doubles your next winning hit; lock in within the first 40% of the timer and win for a **Quick read** (+30%). From the eighth exchange it's **sudden death**: all damage doubles. A typical duel now lasts 10 to 20 seconds.

## Sound
Everything is synthesized live in the browser: formant-filtered voices for battle cries and crowd cheers, war drums with rotating rhythms (faster when an army is charging), a din of steel that swells with the fighting, and a valley reverb over it all.

## Controls
- **Desktop:** drag to orbit, right-drag or WASD to pan, Q/E to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom (far enough to see the whole table). 1-4 orders, 5 seek duel (press again to stop), 6 withdraw, Space aims your spell (click the field to fire, Esc cancels). In duels J/K/L = Strike/Guard/Power. Number keys pick a general.
- **Quest:** grip to grab the table, both grips to scale and turn it. Left stick slides the battlefield, right stick turns and zooms, B resets. Point the right controller to aim your spell and pull the right trigger to fire (otherwise it orders a charge). Left trigger cycles orders, A seeks a duel (again to stop), X twice withdraws. When the enemy hunts your general, a war horn sounds and the controllers buzz. In duels the table glides in close and a scoreboard floats above the fighters: right trigger Strike, left trigger Guard, A/X Power. When choosing a general: left trigger next, right trigger deploy.

Dragon Emperor I not required.

## Debug log

If something breaks, open **Debug log** on the title screen (or the **Log** button in the view bar). It keeps the last 400 entries in the browser, even through crashes and VR sessions: errors with stack traces, warnings, battle and campaign milestones, XR session start/end, WebGL context loss, and a heartbeat every 30 seconds with frame rate, unit and particle counts. **Copy** or **Download .txt** and send it over. A single bad frame no longer freezes the game: the error is logged and the next frame carries on. A red toast appears on desktop when an error is logged. Once a second the game also saves a snapshot of what it is doing (state, unit and effect counts, settings and the last ten on-screen events, plus the last four log lines); for 6 seconds after every button press and every screen change (deploy, taunt, duel, round end, results, war table) the game also saves a step-by-step trace of each frame; a **VR close-ups** checkbox on the title screen can switch off the zoom-in for taunts (and soften it for duels) as a crash test; key steps (every trigger press, choosing a general, deploying, round end, results, returning to the war table) are also saved instantly, so the next load can report the last step the game reached before the tab died; if the tab dies without closing normally, the next load logs it as an ERROR with that snapshot. Frames that stall for more than 1.5 seconds are logged too. A background watchdog notices when the game stops producing frames for 3 seconds and records which part of the frame it was in, so a freeze shows up on the next load as "Previous session froze … while running: <stage>". Heartbeats and crash snapshots also carry GPU stats (geometries, textures, shader programs), live sound voices, uptime and the slowest frame. To keep the Quest's GPU calm, the VR HUD uploads at most 15 times a second, the VR results board at most 12, crowd cheers and battle cries are rendered once when sound starts and replayed as recordings (a live cheer used to start two or three hundred audio nodes in one frame), sounds are capped at 56 at once, and Ludicrous gore allows about three big blood bursts a second.
